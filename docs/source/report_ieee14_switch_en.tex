\begin{abstract}
This standalone report consolidates the IEEE 14-bus study of automatic grid-following (GFL)
$\leftrightarrow$ grid-forming (GFM) mode switching driven by the Adaptive Grid-Support Index
(AGSI++). The test system has \textbf{one synchronous generator} at bus~1 (Padiyar model-1.1,
\emph{manual field, no AVR}, 4-state) sharing a \emph{meshed single-area} network with
\textbf{four GFL IBRs} at buses~2, 3, 6 and 8 (reduced-6 \texttt{SwitchableIbr6}). The device
models are identical to those of the companion Padiyar two-area switching study
\cite{eecon49en}. The composite small-signal model has \textbf{28 states} and \textbf{zero
right-half-plane eigenvalues} ($\max\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)\approx0$); the least-damped mode is
$f=0.345$~Hz with $\zeta=+0.267$, so the operating point is \emph{small-signal stable} and
satisfies the no-AVR / no-PSS, 1-SG + 4-IBR constraint. The report brings together (A)~an SSSA
stability sweep over inertia and internal-reactance (coupling) scaling, (B)~a fault
switching-outcome map over fault severity and location, and (C)~the power-flow operating point,
the SSSA verdict, the time-domain SG trip/reclose switching cycle, and the AGSI++ weight-tuning
provenance. All production dynamics are project-owned base MATLAB (audited \texttt{lu}/\texttt{eig}
primitives only); external programs are never numerical acceptance targets.
\end{abstract}

\section{Models and the AGSI++ switching index}
\label{sec:models}
\paragraph{Switching index.} The AGSI++ merges six normalised per-IBR sub-indices into one
dimensionless index, evaluated every step:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&J_V=\frac{|V-V_{ref}|}{0.10},\quad
J_f=\frac{|f-60|}{0.50},\quad
J_R=\frac{|\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}t|}{1.0},\\
&J_P=\frac{|P_{ref}-P|}{0.20},\quad
J_{SCR}=\max\!\Big(0,\tfrac{3}{SCR}-1\Big),\quad
J_{lock}=\frac{|v_q|}{0.10},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AGSI}=w_V J_V + w_f J_f + w_R J_R + w_P J_P + w_{SCR}J_{SCR} + w_{lock}J_{lock},
\end{equation}
$V$ is the local PCC voltage magnitude ($V_{ref}=1$~pu); $f$ is PLL-estimated frequency in GFL
and virtual-rotor frequency in GFM ($f_0=60$~Hz); $\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}t$ is the accepted-sample
backward-difference RoCoF, low-pass filtered in AGSI++; $P$ is injected active power on the system
base with generator-positive sign and $P_{ref}$ its command; $SCR$ is the diagnostic local
short-circuit ratio; and $v_q$ is the active-frame q-axis voltage (PLL frame in GFL, virtual-rotor
frame in GFM). The denominators are frozen normalisation bases, not protection limits: $J_V=1$,
for example, means a $0.10$~pu voltage deviation. Sub-indices and the aggregate are dimensionless,
are not probabilities, are not clipped to $[0,1]$, and may exceed one.
with weights (sum $=1$) $[w_V,w_f,w_R,w_P,w_{SCR},w_{lock}]=[0.25,0.25,0.15,0.10,0.15,0.10]$.
Switching uses hysteresis and dwell: GFL$\to$GFM when $\mathrm{AGSI}\ge\Gamma_{on}=0.65$ (held
$\ge T_{d,on}$); GFM$\to$GFL when $\mathrm{AGSI}<\Gamma_{off}=0.35$ (held $\ge T_{d,off}$). AGSI++
low-pass filters the RoCoF term ($\tau_R=0.05$~s) to reject the one-sample RoCoF spike at a
topology change, and adds the weak-grid strength term $J_{SCR}$ and the PLL loss-of-lock proxy
$J_{lock}=|v_q|/0.10$.

\paragraph{Code-level switching mechanism.}
Each IBR is an independent hybrid two-state machine with committed mode
$m_i\in\{0,1\}$ ($0=$GFL, $1=$GFM), not an online optimiser or a continuous blend of the two
models. Its own AGSI++ and dwell timers determine the normal transitions, so devices switch at the
same simulated time only when their local conditions mature together. The IEEE14 run has
\texttt{gra\_override=false}: fault/trip transitions are purely index-driven. A synchronised SG
reclose is the explicit exception---the event driver performs a coordinated reference handback to
GFL without waiting for $\Gamma_{off}$; the index supervisor then resumes immediately.

\paragraph{Reduced-6 IBR models.} \textbf{GFL} state order
$x=[\,i_d,\ i_q,\ \delta_{\mathrm{PLL}},\ \xi_{\mathrm{PLL}},\ \xi_P,\ \xi_Q\,]$ (L-filter dq
currents, SRF-PLL angle and integrator, and $P/Q$ outer-loop integrators). \textbf{GFM} state
order $x=[\,i_d,\ i_q,\ \omega,\ \delta,\ E,\ \xi_V\,]$ (currents, virtual-rotor speed and angle,
internal voltage magnitude, and d-axis voltage integrator), with cross-paired terminals
$v_{gd}=E+X i_q$, $v_{gq}=-X i_d$. Each IBR is one \texttt{SwitchableIbr6} device wrapping both
branches; the active state is always 6-dimensional and a switch is \emph{bumpless} --- the target
branch is re-initialised to the instantaneous terminal $(V,P,Q)$ so the injected current is
continuous,
\begin{equation}
I^{+}=\overline{\left(\tfrac{S}{V}\right)}=I^{-}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Synchronous generator (Padiyar model-1.1, manual, 4-state).} State order
$[\delta,\ \omega,\ E_q',\ E_d']$:
\begin{equation}
\dot\delta=\omega_B(\omega-1),\qquad
\dot\omega=\frac{P_m-(\omega-1)/R-T_e-D(\omega-1)}{2H},
\end{equation}
with $E_{fd}=E_{fd0}$ constant (no AVR),
$\dot E_q'=[E_{fd}-E_q'-(X_d-X_d')I_d]/T_{d0}'$ and $\dot E_d'=[-E_d'+(X_q-X_q')I_q]/T_{q0}'$.
The manual (no-AVR, no-PSS) machine keeps the composite free of any excitation-control state, as
required by the study constraint. Full derivations, the stator solution, and the network DAE are
in the companion report.

\section{Test system}
\label{sec:system}
Figure~\ref{fig:oneline} is the single-line diagram of the study system: the IEEE 14-bus network
with the synchronous generator \textbf{SG$_1$} at bus~1 acting as slack (angle reference) and four
inverter-based resources \textbf{IBR$_1$--IBR$_4$} at buses~2, 3, 6 and 8, each backed by an energy
source and/or storage. In steady state every IBR is dispatched as a PQ (grid-following) unit; when
the AGSI++ index commands a switch, the affected unit becomes a voltage-forming (PV / grid-forming)
source. The remaining buses carry the standard IEEE 14-bus loads, and the transformer taps of
branches 4--7, 4--9 and 5--6 are represented exactly by the tap-aware admittance matrix taken from
the in-house power flow. This is the same one-line arrangement used in the EECON49-P4 study
\cite{eecon49en}, which is why the device mapping (1~SG + 4~IBR at the original generator buses) is
kept unchanged here.

\section{Power-flow operating point}
\label{sec:pf}
The base case is solved by the in-house Newton--Raphson power flow. The synchronous generator at
bus~1 carries the bulk dispatch, $P_{g1}=2.324$~pu $=232.4$~MW (slack / angle reference). The IBR
at bus~2 dispatches $P_{g}=0.40$~pu; the IBRs at buses~3, 6 and 8 are on voltage/reactive support
duty with scheduled $P_g=0$. Load is distributed over the remaining IEEE~14-bus buses. This
operating point is the initial condition for both the small-signal linearisation
(\S\ref{sec:sssa}) and every time-domain run (\S\ref{sec:ts}); the pre-event active-power panel
(Fig.~\ref{fig:p}) shows the SG holding $\approx2.32$~pu and IBR1 holding $\approx0.40$~pu,
confirming the dispatch.

\section{Small-signal stability and the stability sweep}
\label{sec:sssa}
Linearising the composite 28-state model about the base case gives \textbf{zero right-half-plane
eigenvalues}: $\max\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)\approx0$ (only the $\approx0$ angle-reference gauge
mode, at the $10^{-8}$ level), and the least-damped oscillatory mode is $f=0.345$~Hz with
$\zeta=+0.267$. The meshed single-area IEEE~14-bus operating point is therefore
\emph{small-signal stable}.

To test how robust this verdict is, two one-parameter sweeps re-derive the equilibrium
consistently and re-linearise at each point (Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}, Table~\ref{tab:sweep}): an
\emph{inertia} sweep on $H_{\times}$ (scaling SG $H$ and damping) and a \emph{coupling}
sweep on $k_X$ (scaling SG internal reactances; larger $=$ weaker coupling). The
inertia sweep is essentially flat and stable across $H_\times\in[0.02,2]$
($\max\operatorname{Re}\sim10^{-8}$, $\zeta\approx0.267$, mode near $0.345$~Hz). The coupling
sweep stays stable across $k_X\in[1,20]$: damping erodes \emph{monotonically}
($\zeta:0.267\to0.150$ at $20\times$ internal reactance) but never crosses $\zeta=0$, and
$\max\operatorname{Re}$ stays at the $10^{-8}$ gauge level. \textbf{No instability boundary is
found on either knob.}

\paragraph{Interpretation (topology, not inertia or coupling).} The contrast with the Padiyar
two-area system ($1$~SG $+3$~IBR, weak inter-area tie) is decisive: that operating point has one
right-half-plane eigenvalue, $\max\operatorname{Re}=+0.058\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$ ($\zeta=+0.122$,
$f=0.677$~Hz), i.e.\ \emph{small-signal unstable}, whereas the meshed IEEE~14-bus stays stable
even at $20\times$ internal reactance or $0.02\times$ inertia. The instability of the two-area
case is therefore \textbf{topological} --- it comes from the weak inter-area tie, not from low
inertia or coupling strength, which a meshed grid tolerates.

\section{Time-domain switching: SG trip and reclose}
\label{sec:ts}
The demonstration run applies, over a $50$~s window: a temporary three-phase fault $Z_f=j0.1$~pu
at bus~4 during $1.0$--$1.15$~s, an \textbf{SG trip at $6$~s}, and a synchronised \textbf{SG
reclose at $9$~s}. The two disturbances are deliberately \emph{well separated}: the fault is
cleared $4.85$~s before the breaker opens, so the post-fault transient has fully decayed and the
IBR response to the trip is not contaminated by the fault. (This differs from the shorter $6$~s
objective run used for weight tuning in \S\ref{sec:tune}, which trips at $1$~s and recloses at
$4$~s.) Figures \ref{fig:agsi}--\ref{fig:p} show the AGSI++ per IBR, the bus voltages, the
frequencies, and the active-power injections. The ordered timeline is:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item $[0,1)$~s --- SG is the slack; all four IBRs run as GFL at their scheduled dispatch.
\item $1.0$--$1.15$~s --- temporary fault $Z_f=j0.1$~pu; the IBRs \emph{ride through} it as GFL
(AGSI spikes but the fault clears and no IBR reaches the dwell-confirmed $\Gamma_{on}$ crossing).
\item $1.15$--$6$~s --- fault-free recovery; the trajectory settles back to the SG-slack operating
point before the next event, so the trip is a clean, independent disturbance.
\item $t=6$~s --- \textbf{SG trips}. All four IBRs cross $\Gamma_{on}$ ($\mathrm{AGSI}\approx
1.88$--$2.07$) and \textbf{form GFM} after the $T_{d,on}=0.5$~s dwell, i.e.\ at $t=6.5$~s, so the
island survives (network minimum voltage $\approx0.49$--$0.53$~pu on the formed island;
$\min|V|=0.453$~pu at the trip transient).
\item $t=9$~s --- \textbf{synchronised reclose}; the angle reference is handed back to the SG and
all four IBRs revert to their scheduled GFL dispatch. The reclose transient briefly re-triggers
forming at $t=9.5$~s, and AGSI++ peaks bounded at $\approx3.5$.
\item $t\to50$~s --- the response \emph{stays bounded}: voltages recover ($V_{end}\approx0.941$~pu
at the weakest bus, higher elsewhere), the frequency excursion peaks near $61.4$~Hz and then
settles at $\approx59.6$~Hz (primary droop is present, but there is no secondary/integral frequency
restoration to return exactly to $60$~Hz), the SG re-takes
$\approx2.53$~pu, and three of the four IBRs revert to GFL as their index falls below
$\Gamma_{off}$ at $t\approx22.3$--$23.1$~s (IBR2 at bus~3 remains GFM, supporting the weakest
node). AGSI++ stays bounded ($\approx3.5$ peak) with no chatter.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Fault switching-outcome map}
\label{sec:map}
The switching map is a fault-only sweep (\emph{no} SG trip, $T=4$~s): a temporary three-phase
shunt fault during $1.0$--$1.15$~s is swept over fault reactance and fault bus,
\[
X_f\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.20,0.30,0.50\}~\text{pu},\qquad \text{fault bus}\in\{3,4,5,9\},
\]
and each run is classified into one of four outcome regions (Fig.~\ref{fig:map},
Table~\ref{tab:map}): \emph{ride-through} (stayed GFL, no switch), \emph{index-switch} (AGSI++
crosses $\Gamma_{on}$ so $\ge1$ IBR forms GFM then reverts), \emph{diverge / no post-clear
recovery} (the run diverged, or the minimum voltage \emph{measured after the fault is cleared}
stays below the $0.25$~pu collapse floor), and \emph{non-converged} (fail-closed: the corrector
did not converge, so no physical outcome is claimed). The collapse test deliberately uses the
\textbf{post-clear} minimum voltage: while a low-impedance shunt fault is applied the faulted bus
is pulled down \emph{by construction}, so the during-fault sag carries no information about
survival. Over the 24 fault points the tally is \textbf{ride-through $=2$, index-switch $=22$,
diverge $=0$, non-converged $=0$}: every swept fault is cleared successfully and the network
recovers to $0.98$--$1.01$~pu, with the AGSI++ supervisor forming GFM on all but the two mildest
points.

\paragraph{Physics of the map.} Once the collapse test is applied where it belongs --- after
clearing --- a clean \emph{switching} gradient emerges rather than a collapse region. Every one of
the 24 faults is survived: the deepest during-fault sags reach $\min|V|=0.075$--$0.14$~pu (bolted-ish
$X_f=0.02$), yet after the breaker clears at $1.15$~s the network recovers to $0.98$--$0.99$~pu, so
no point is a voltage collapse and every Newton step converged. What the severity actually decides
is \emph{whether the AGSI++ supervisor commands forming}: from $X_f=0.02$ up to $0.30$ at every bus
(and up to $0.50$ at IBR bus~3 and the electrically weak bus~9) the sag pushes AGSI++ over
$\Gamma_{on}$, so at least one IBR forms GFM and then reverts $\Rightarrow$ \emph{index-switch}
(22 points, $2$--$10$ transitions each). Only the two mildest cases ($X_f=0.50$ at the stiffer load
buses~4 and 5) stay GFL with zero switches $\Rightarrow$ \emph{ride-through}. Fault location
matters as expected: at the IBR bus~3 and the weak bus~9 even the mildest $X_f$ triggers switching,
whereas the stiffer load buses~4/5 do not. The load-step sub-table confirms the interpretation:
permanent $+10/30/50\%$ load steps at bus~4 (no SG trip)
all ride through with min$|V|\ge0.999$~pu and zero switches --- a load step is far milder than a
fault and never drives an IBR to form.

\section{Event coverage and present model limits}
\label{sec:limits}
The existing evidence covers: no-event hold (no false switch); mild cleared faults (ride-through);
stronger/weak-bus faults (22 of 24 points index-switch and return, with all 24 recovering); permanent
$+10/30/50\%$ load steps at bus~4 (no switch); SG trip (index-driven forming); and SG reclose
(coordinated handback followed by index-driven re-forming/recovery). A switch is not required for a
test to pass: retaining GFL under a disturbance that never satisfies threshold and dwell is the
correct supervisor outcome.

The claims are bounded by the current model: positive-sequence RMS and balanced three-phase shunt
faults, not EMT/PWM/harmonic or unbalanced-fault simulation; reduced-6 IBR branches without a full
DC-link, LCL, SOC, thermal, vendor-protection or LVRT/anti-windup stack; a study-level $1.2$-rated
current-magnitude limiter; a manual-field 4-state SG (no AVR/PSS, primary droop but no secondary
frequency restoration); and constant-impedance dynamic loads. The scheduled reclose is made
synchronous by re-initialisation and does not model a synchronism-check relay or failed close.
Bumpless transfer proves $I^+=I^-$ only at the switching instant, not zero subsequent voltage,
frequency or power transient. AGSI++ weights, thresholds and dwells are frozen diagnostic study
choices, not certified protection settings; online BO/MPC, measurement noise/latency and broad
parameter/topology uncertainty are not covered. Non-converged trajectories fail closed and support
no physical claim.

These statements were checked against the runtime mode/timer/transfer implementation in
\texttt{SwitchableIbr6}, the accepted-step and coordinated-reclose path in
\texttt{padiyar\_switch\_tds}, the IEEE14 builder, and the no-event/fault/bidirectional/hysteresis
tests.

\section{AGSI++ weight-tuning provenance}
\label{sec:tune}
The hand-set AGSI++ weights and thresholds were checked against an in-house, offline, design-time
search (\texttt{scripts/reporting/optimize\_agsi\_weights.m}; project-owned base MATLAB,
\textbf{no external optimization solver}, not on any production runtime path). Guided by the
EECON49-P4 Bayesian-Optimization \emph{concept}, the search is a uniform-simplex random sampling
of the six weights (each $\ge0$, sum $=1$) plus $\Gamma_{on}/\Gamma_{off}$, followed by a
coordinate local refine, minimising, for the IEEE~14-bus SG-trip@$1$~s $+$ reclose@$4$~s run over
$T=6$~s:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J = {}& 12\cdot\overline{|1-V_{\min}(t)|} \;+\; 0.6\cdot(\text{total mode switches})\\
&{}+\; 0.05\cdot\max\mathrm{AGSI}\quad(+\,10^{5}\ \text{penalty on divergence}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The search improves the objective by only \textbf{$0.4\%$} ($J:11.959\to11.906$), i.e.\ the
hand-set weights and thresholds are \emph{already near-optimal} for this stable operating point.
The optimum leans toward the grid-strength term ($w_{SCR}$) and drops $w_{lock}$, consistent with
weak-grid GFL instability being the dominant stress, but the gain is negligible and the hand-set
values are more physically interpretable and balanced. \textbf{Decision:} keep the frozen defaults
$[w_V,w_f,w_R,w_P,w_{SCR},w_{lock}]=[0.25,0.25,0.15,0.10,0.15,0.10]$, $\Gamma_{on}=0.65$,
$\Gamma_{off}=0.35$; the search is retained only as a reproducible tool that \emph{validates} the
choice (\texttt{ASSUMED\_DIAGNOSTIC}; frozen before use).

\section{Summary}
On the meshed IEEE~14-bus system, one manual (no-AVR) synchronous generator and four GFL IBRs form
a small-signal-stable operating point (28 states, zero RHP eigenvalues, least-damped $0.345$~Hz at
$\zeta=+0.267$) that stays stable across the whole inertia and coupling sweep; the AGSI++
supervisor rides through a temporary fault as GFL, forms GFM on the SG trip to hold the island, and
hands the reference back on reclose while staying bounded and chatter-free. The switching-outcome
map shows that all 24 swept faults are cleared and recovered (no collapse point), with fault
severity and location deciding only whether AGSI++ commands forming (22 index-switch, 2
ride-through), and the offline weight search confirms the hand-set AGSI++ weights are near-optimal and
frozen. The stark contrast with the small-signal-unstable Padiyar two-area case
($\max\operatorname{Re}=+0.058\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$) shows the difference is topological.
