\title{\textbf{IEEE 14-Bus GFL$\leftrightarrow$GFM Switching with Project AGSI++}\\
\large Production all-KCL 200-s switching and SG-reference handback}
\date{6 August 2026}
\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
One six-state EMF6 synchronous generator (SG) and four registered full-state dual-mode IBRs are
simulated by the project-owned fixed-step, all-KCL hybrid engine for 200~s. The exact chronology is
SG trip at 20~s, load increase at 50~s, bus-9 fault at 85--85.15~s, line 6--13 trip at 110~s,
topology restoration and SG-close request at 145~s, and endpoint at 200~s. The operating point uses
a $1.00$~pu slack reference with a $\pm5\%$ admissible voltage band. The run converges with maximum
accepted-step residual $9.685\times10^{-9}$ and at most two subdivision levels. The synchronism
guard accepts the SG at 145.400~s without changing any IBR mode. Each still-GFM IBR then returns to
GFL only after the implemented two-term severity remains below its release threshold for the full
down-line dwell and the atomic transfer/KCL transaction passes at 146.400~s. At 200~s,
$f_{COI}=60.000008$~Hz and the all-bus voltage range is 0.966733--1.057534~pu; no bus is below
0.95~pu, while bus 6 remains above the 1.05 reporting band as disclosed.
No Bayesian-optimisation controller is used. A seeded band-limited display ripple is drawn beside,
not instead of, each raw trace and affects figures only.
\end{abstract}

\section{Case, decision index, and switching semantics}
\label{sec:case-bus-roles}
The system base is 100~MVA and 60~Hz. The SG is at bus 1; IBR1--IBR4 are at buses 2, 3, 6, and 8.
The buses carry a parallel \texttt{bus\_role} label
(\texttt{REF}/\texttt{PV}/\texttt{PQ}/\texttt{GFM}/\texttt{GFL}) that is
\emph{presentation-only} and never feeds the power-flow equations
(Section~\ref{sec:case-bus-roles}): the SG bus 1 is REF, buses 2/3/6/8 (the
IBRs) are GFL resources on this operating point, and the remaining load buses
are PQ. The numerical internal bus type (REF$=1$, PV$=2$, PQ$=3$) in the
12-column \texttt{bus\_data} is unchanged by this descriptor. In particular,
\emph{GFL does not mean PV}: GFL is a dynamic current-controller mode, whereas
PV/PQ denotes steady-state variable ownership. The four GFLs in this case
receive specified $P_{ref},Q_{ref}$ and are therefore PQ resources; $|V|$ is a
network-solved output. A PV resource would instead specify $P,|V|$ and let $Q$
be solved by a voltage controller.
The per-IBR switching supervisor of this study is the two-term severity index implemented in
\texttt{ibr.SwitchableIbr6} ($\texttt{index\_mode}=\texttt{"agsi\_pp"}$):
\begin{equation}
S_i=\min\!\left(1,\max\!\left(0,\;w_VJ_V+w_fJ_f\right)\right),\qquad
J_V=\frac{|V-V_{ref,i}|}{\Delta V_{base}},\quad
J_f=\frac{|f-f_0|}{\Delta f_{base}},
\label{eq:agsi-legacy}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta V_{base}=0.10$~pu, $\Delta f_{base}=0.50$~Hz, weights $w_V=w_f=0.5$ (the
$0.30{:}0.30$ source ratio renormalised), and $V_{ref,i}$ the \emph{healthy per-bus} voltage
from the pre-fault SG-online power flow (Section~\ref{sec:case-bus-roles}). The five former
weighted stresses (RoCoF, power tracking, SCR, PLL-lock, and GRA) have zero weight in this preset.
They remain diagnostics where available, but the implementation does \emph{not} claim that all five
are hidden mode-change gates. An equal $1/7$ average would dilute $J_V,J_f$ with mostly-zero terms
and could hide a real voltage/frequency deviation.

The SG trip at 20~s is a \texttt{CASE\_DEFINED} atomic all-GFM transaction. SG reclose is different:
Phase~1 closes only after the synchronism guard and returns reference ownership to the SG while the
IBR modes remain unchanged. Phase~2 then evaluates each still-GFM IBR independently and authorises a
GFM$\to$GFL transfer only after its severity stays below $\Gamma_{off}$ for $T_{d,off}$. Each authorised
transfer still requires the device reset map and a right-limit full-KCL solve to pass. The defaults
are $\Gamma_{on}=0.65$, $\Gamma_{off}=0.35$, $T_{d,on}=0.10$~s, and $T_{d,off}=1.00$~s.

\subsection{Implemented two-term decision index and separate transaction gates}
\label{sec:index-redesign}
The switching decision asks whether the voltage/frequency reference is healthy, so the continuous
severity contains only those two quantities:
\begin{equation}
S_i=\min\!\left(1,\max\!\left(0,\;w_VJ_V+w_fJ_f\right)\right),\qquad
J_V=\frac{|V-V_{ref}|}{\Delta V_{base}},\quad
J_f=\frac{|f-f_0|}{\Delta f_{base}},
\label{eq:severity}
\end{equation}
with $\Delta V_{base}=0.10$~pu, $\Delta f_{base}=0.50$~Hz, and $w_V=w_f=0.5$, $w_V+w_f=1$. The two
bases are \texttt{SOURCE\_DEFINED}; the equal split follows the source ratio $0.30:0.30$
renormalised, so no free weight remains.

The reduced-6 local supervisor applies the hysteresis/dwell law directly,
\begin{align}
\text{GFL}\to\text{GFM}:\;\;&S_i\ge\Gamma_{on}\quad\text{for }T_{d,on},\notag\\
\text{GFM}\to\text{GFL}:\;\;&S_i<\Gamma_{off}\quad\text{for }T_{d,off}.
\label{eq:switch-law}
\end{align}
The composite all-KCL handback uses the same two-term down-line but has explicit system-level
prerequisites rather than undocumented AGSI terms: the SG must already own the reference; the
healthy-PF voltage profile must map uniquely to each IBR bus; finite inertia-weighted COI frequency
evidence must exist; and the device transfer plus right-limit KCL transaction must pass. Missing
voltage/frequency evidence resets the dwell timer and releases nothing. After a partial release,
all remaining timers reset because the mode transfer changes their voltage/frequency environment.
A design-time weight tuner exists at
\path{scripts/reporting/optimize_agsi_weights.m} (\texttt{NUMERICAL\_METHOD}); its output was not
used, and the source $0.30{:}0.30$ renormalisation leaves no free weight to tune.

\subsubsection{Why $J_V,J_f$ suffice as severity, and why the weights are $0.5{:}0.5$}
\label{sec:agsi-weight-rationale}
So that the design can be defended when asked ``why only $J_V,J_f$'' and ``why these weights,''
the reasoning is stated in three parts.

\textbf{(1) Why $J_V$ and $J_f$ suffice as the severity.} The objective of switching is the single
question \emph{``is the reference healthy?''}, and the two quantities a grid-forming reference
directly regulates are the voltage magnitude $|V|$ and the frequency $f$ (voltage through the
voltage PI and the $Q$--$V$ droop; frequency through the virtual rotor and the droop). The severity
therefore has to measure ``distance from the reference'' with exactly these two quantities. Every
other term of~\eqref{eq:agsi-legacy} is not a reference-severity but a condition of the
surrounding system.

\textbf{(2) Why the other five terms are not severity weights.} Quantities such as SCR, current
margin, or PLL health are conditions or diagnostics, not distances from the voltage/frequency
reference: there is no physics in which a good SCR compensates for over-current. Active/reactive
power tracking has the same non-compensatory role for a GFL handback: good $P$ tracking must not
cancel bad $Q$ tracking, and good voltage/frequency must not hide a dispatch mismatch. Hence
$J_P,J_Q$ are not added as weighted severity terms. Where a source or case requirement supplies
tracking bands, $|P-P^\star|$ and $|Q-Q^\star|$ belong to separate release-readiness gates; the
case-defined $P^\star,Q^\star$ must in all cases be restored consistently before transfer. In this
implementation the former AGSI weights are zero; only separately implemented plant,
event-authority, transfer-map, reference-ownership, and KCL checks may block a transaction. A
concrete count from
\eqref{eq:agsi-legacy}: with the $1/7$ average a single term can trigger the threshold
$\Gamma_{on}=0.65$ only once it reaches $7\times0.65=4.55$, i.e.\ $J_V=4.55$ back-transforms to
$|\Delta V|=4.55\times0.10=0.455$~pu --- a catastrophic voltage collapse --- yet the $1/7$ index
reads it merely as ``at threshold.'' The index therefore cannot distinguish ``one severe term''
from ``all terms mildly stressed,'' and does not say what is actually wrong. The two-term severity
always saturates at $1$, so the threshold $0.65$ reads directly as ``the normalized deviation
reaches $65\%$ of the band,'' which is interpretable at a glance.

\textbf{(3) Why the weights are $0.5{:}0.5$.} Separate the bases from the weights. The bases
($\Delta V_{base}=0.10$~pu and $\Delta f_{base}=0.50$~Hz) make $J$ dimensionless and comparable:
$J_V=1$ means a full $10\%$ band deviation and $J_f=1$ means a full $0.5$~Hz band deviation. The
weight then states whether the two normalised deviations are equally important. The source
EECON49-P4 Sec.~4.1 assigns the weight ratio of $V$ to $f$ as $0.30{:}0.30$, i.e.\ it treats them
as equally important; renormalising to unit sum gives exactly $0.5{:}0.5$. \textbf{The weights are
therefore pinned by normalisation from the source, not tuned.} If asked ``why not $0.7{:}0.3$,''
the answer is: changing them would need source support, and the source treats $V$ and $f$ as
equal. This is the provenance difference from the legacy $1/7$ weights: $1/7$ has no source
support and cannot be defended, whereas $0.5{:}0.5$ can be defended to the end.

\section{Dynamic equations}
\label{sec:dyn}
The operational SG state is
$x_{SG}=[\delta,\Delta\omega,E'_q,E'_d,E''_q,E''_d]^T$:
\begin{align}
\dot\delta&=\omega_0\Delta\omega,&
2H\Delta\dot\omega&=T_m-T_e-D\Delta\omega,\\
T'_{d0}\dot E'_q&=E_{fd}+c_dE''_q-d_dE'_q,&
T'_{q0}\dot E'_d&=c_qE''_d-d_qE'_d,\\
T''_{d0}\dot E''_q&=E'_q-E''_q-(X'_d-X''_d)I_d,&
T''_{q0}\dot E''_d&=E'_d-E''_d+(X'_q-X''_q)I_q.
\end{align}
Here $T_m$ and $T_e$ are the mechanical input and air-gap electrical torque on the common
per-unit base; $T_e$ equals air-gap electrical power at synchronous speed. The source singular limit
$T'_{q0}=0$ freezes $E'_d=0$ exactly. Two prime-mover states remain outside the six-state
electromagnetic order:
\[
T_{SV}\dot P_{SV}=-P_{SV}+C,\qquad
T_{CH}\dot P_m=-P_m+P_{SV}.
\]
For one interval of length $h$, with $C$ held at the accepted left-sample value, define
$e_{SV}=e^{-h/T_{SV}}$ and $e_{CH}=e^{-h/T_{CH}}$. The production update is exact:
\begin{align}
P_{SV}^{+}&=C+(P_{SV}-C)e_{SV},\\
P_m^{+}&=\begin{cases}
C+(P_m-C)e_{CH}+(P_{SV}-C)(h/T_{CH})e_{CH}, & T_{SV}=T_{CH},\\[2pt]
C+(P_m-C)e_{CH}+(P_{SV}-C)\dfrac{T_{SV}}{T_{SV}-T_{CH}}(e_{SV}-e_{CH}), & T_{SV}\ne T_{CH}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:gov-exact}
\end{align}
The EMF6 input is $T_m$: the time step uses the interval-average
$T_m=\tfrac12(P_m+P_m^+)$ in the implicit trapezoidal solve and publishes $P_m^+$ as the next
accepted input. After reclose, $C=\operatorname{sat}(P_{ref}-\Delta\omega/R,P_{min},P_{max})$
implements primary
droop regulation and the two states are initialised bumplessly from the accepted mechanical input.
While the breaker is open, $C$ instead comes from the separate deterministic phase planner; the
planner also supplies the field-matching command. Thus $T_e=0$ in the open-circuit SG equations,
but $P_m$ is not claimed to remain frozen for the whole outage: its input follows this explicitly
\texttt{ASSUMED\_DIAGNOSTIC} synchronising path. Neither path adds secondary AGC, and the numerical
mapping is not a machine-specific governor validation.

\subsection{IBR state superset}
Each IBR carries a constant-dimension \textbf{20-state} switching superset: the 13-state
grid-forming branch (REGFM\_B1, generator model~G2) and the 7-state grid-following branch
(the WECC REGC\_A and REEC\_A pair) exist at all times,
\begin{align}
x_{GFM}={}&[\Delta\omega_m,\delta_{IT},x_w,x_E,\delta_{PLL},x_{PLL},
P_{inv,f},I_{d,f},Q_{inv,f},\notag\\
&\hspace{2.1em}V_{inv,f},I_{q,f},\delta_{IT,max},\delta_{IT,min}]^T,\\
x_{GFL}={}&[V_{t,f},P_f,I_{q,cmd,f},P_{ord},V_{LVPL,f},I_{p,reg},I_{q,reg}]^T,
\end{align}
so that $x_{IBR}=[x_{GFM}^T\;x_{GFL}^T]^T\in\mathbb R^{20}$. All quantities are per-unit on the
device base $M_{base}$; the network interface applies $\kappa=S_{base}/M_{base}$. Powers are
positive into the network. Only the branch selected by the present mode $q$ contributes a nonzero
derivative; the other branch satisfies $\dot x=0$ exactly.

\subsubsection*{PLL ownership is model-specific, not a GFM/GFL definition}
The state lists above can otherwise appear counter-intuitive: this report's GFM has a PLL pair,
whereas its GFL has no explicit PLL state. This is an exact statement about the \emph{two selected
models}, not a universal rule for grid-forming and grid-following control. Their angle contracts are
\begin{align}
\text{REGFM\_B1 GFM:}\quad
v_q&=\Im\!\left(Ve^{-\mathrm j\delta_{PLL}}\right), &
\dot x_{PLL}&=v_q, &
\dot\delta_{PLL}&=\omega_0(k_{p,PLL}v_q+k_{i,PLL}x_{PLL}),\label{eq:pll-scope-gfm}\\
\delta_{VSM}&=\delta_{PLL}+\delta_{IT}, &
\dot\delta_{IT}&=\omega_0\Delta\omega_m-\dot\delta_{PLL}, &
\therefore\quad\dot\delta_{VSM}&=\omega_0\Delta\omega_m,\notag\\
\text{WECC GFL:}\quad
\theta_V&=\operatorname{atan2}(V_y,V_x), &
I&=\frac{(I_{p,reg}\gamma(|V|)-\mathrm jI_{q,reg})e^{\mathrm j\theta_V}}{\kappa}.\label{eq:pll-scope-gfl}
\end{align}
Thus the REGFM\_B1 PLL defines the local $dq$ measurement frame, while the relative state
$\delta_{IT}$ cancels the PLL motion from the final VSM-angle derivative; the virtual swing still
sets $\dot\delta_{VSM}$. The selected WECC aggregate instead applies the measured terminal angle
instantaneously in its algebraic current interface, so it adds no PLL differential equation. This
must not be read as proof that a physical GFL converter has no synchronisation loop: the loop's
dynamics are outside this particular seven-state WECC aggregate.

Counterexamples exist in this same repository. The opt-in 10-state GFL-RMS10 branch carries
$(\delta_{PLL},\xi_{PLL})$, whereas the reduced six-state GFM branch evolves its rotor angle only by
its swing integrator and has no PLL. Neither counterexample is on the trajectory reported here.
Consequently, for the present model pair only,
\[
n_{PLL}^{GFM}=2,\qquad n_{PLL}^{GFL}=0,
\]
while the controller labels ``GFM'' and ``GFL'' alone do not determine either number.

\subsection{Grid-forming branch: 13 states, term by term}
The angle reference is the sum of a phase-locked component and a virtual-machine component, so the
GFM branch \emph{does} own a PLL pair. With
$V=V_x+\mathrm jV_y$ the terminal voltage and $v_q=-V_x\sin\delta_{PLL}+V_y\cos\delta_{PLL}$ its
quadrature error,
\begin{align}
\dot x_{PLL}&=v_q, &
\dot\delta_{PLL}&=\omega_0\left(k_{p,PLL}v_q+k_{i,PLL}x_{PLL}\right),
\label{eq:gfm-pll}
\end{align}
both frozen to zero while $|V|<V_{PLL,frz}$ so that a collapsed terminal cannot drive the tracker.
The virtual synchronous machine supplies the frequency deviation, with a washout state providing
transient-only damping:
\begin{align}
2H\Delta\dot\omega_m&=P_{ref,inv}-P_{inv,f}
-\left(1/m_p+D_1\right)\Delta\omega_m-D_2\left(\Delta\omega_m-x_w\right),\label{eq:gfm-swing}\\
\dot x_w&=\omega_D\left(\Delta\omega_m-x_w\right).\label{eq:gfm-washout}
\end{align}
Here $1/m_p$ is the droop gain, $D_1$ the steady damping, and $D_2$ acts only on the
high-pass component $\Delta\omega_m-x_w$. The internal angle is stored \emph{relative} to the PLL,
which is what keeps the two angle sources consistent:
\begin{equation}
\dot\delta_{IT}=\omega_0\Delta\omega_m-\dot\delta_{PLL},\qquad
\delta_{VSM}=\delta_{PLL}+\delta_{IT},\label{eq:gfm-angle}
\end{equation}
with $\dot\delta_{IT}$ held at zero whenever $\delta_{IT}$ sits on the active bound of
$[\delta_{IT,min},\delta_{IT,max}]$ and the derivative would push it further out.
The voltage magnitude comes from a PI regulator with reactive droop and anti-windup,
\begin{align}
\dot x_E&=V_{ref}-V_{inv,f},&
E_{VSM}^{raw}&=V_{ref}-m_qQ_{inv,f}+k_{pv}\left(V_{ref}-V_{inv,f}\right)+k_{iv}x_E,
\label{eq:gfm-vpi}
\end{align}
where $E_{VSM}=\mathrm{sat}\left(E_{VSM}^{raw},E_{min},E_{max}\right)$ and $\dot x_E$ is set to
zero when the limiter is saturated in the direction of further integration. The limits $E_{min}$
and $E_{max}$ are \emph{not constants}: they are recomputed every step from the current state so
that the $q$-axis current cannot exceed its limit,
\begin{align}
E_{min}&=\sqrt{\left(V_{inv,f}-I_{q,max}^{SS}X_L\right)^2+\left(I_{d,f}X_L\right)^2},\\
E_{max}&=\sqrt{\left(V_{inv,f}+I_{q,max}^{SS}X_L\right)^2+\left(I_{d,f}X_L\right)^2},
\label{eq:gfm-elim}
\end{align}
with the current limit split by \texttt{PQFlag}: on $d$-axis priority
$I_{d,max}^{SS}=k_fI_{max}^{SS}$ and
\[
I_{q,max}^{SS}=\sqrt{\max\!\left(\left(I_{max}^{SS}\right)^2-I_{d,f}^2,\,0\right)},
\]
and the reverse assignment on $q$-axis priority. The $x_E$ anti-windup uses the same conditional-hold rule: $\dot
x_E=0$ when $E_{VSM}^{raw}$ is outside the band and the derivative still pushes outward.
The current-angle bounds are themselves states, integrating the mismatch between the steady-state
$d$-axis current limit and the measured $d$-axis current, so that the angle authority contracts
when the device is overloaded:
\begin{align}
\dot\delta_{IT,max}^{raw}&=k_I\left(I_{d,max}^{SS}-I_{d,f}\right),&
\dot\delta_{IT,min}^{raw}&=k_I\left(-K_eI_{d,max}^{SS}-I_{d,f}\right),
\label{eq:gfm-bounds}
\end{align}
with the states constrained to $\delta_{IT,max}\in[0,\delta_{max}]$ and
$\delta_{IT,min}\in[-\delta_{max},0]$. Each derivative is replaced by the same conditional hold on
its own bound, with
$\delta_{max}=\arcsin\!\left(X_LI_{max}^{SS}\right)$ and $\dot\delta_{IT,min}=0$ when the
energy-storage flag is inactive. The five remaining states are first-order measurement filters,
\begin{align}
T_{pf}\dot P_{inv,f}&=\kappa P-P_{inv,f},&
T_{If}\dot I_{d,f}&=\kappa I_d-I_{d,f},&
T_{Qf}\dot Q_{inv,f}&=\kappa Q-Q_{inv,f},\\
T_{Vf}\dot V_{inv,f}&=|V|-V_{inv,f},&
T_{If}\dot I_{q,f}&=\kappa I_q-I_{q,f},&&
\end{align}
where $P+\mathrm jQ=V\overline{I_{out}}$ and $(I_d,I_q)$ are the injected current projected on the
PLL frame. The network injection is voltage-behind-impedance with circular saturation:
\begin{equation}
I_{out}=\begin{cases}
I_{unc}, & |I_{unc}|<I_{max}^F/\kappa,\\[2pt]
\dfrac{I_{max}^F}{\kappa}\dfrac{I_{unc}}{|I_{unc}|}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
I_{unc}=\frac{E_{VSM}e^{\mathrm j\delta_{VSM}}-V}{\kappa\left(R_e+\mathrm jX_L\right)}.
\label{eq:gfm-current}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Grid-following branch: 7 states, term by term}
The GFL branch is the registered WECC pair and is a \emph{current-source} model: it owns no PLL
state because it aligns algebraically to the measured terminal-voltage angle. Its injection is
\begin{equation}
I=\frac{\left(I_{p,reg}\,\gamma(|V|)-\mathrm jI_{q,reg}\right)e^{\mathrm j\angle V}}{\kappa},
\label{eq:gfl-current}
\end{equation}
with $\gamma$ the REGC\_A low-voltage-point gain and a high-voltage reactive clamp applied above
$V_{olim}$. The states are three measurement filters, the power order, and the two regulated
currents:
\begin{align}
T_{rv}\dot V_{t,f}&=|V|-V_{t,f},&
T_{p}\dot P_f&=\kappa P-P_f,\\
T_{fltr}\dot V_{LVPL,f}&=|V|-V_{LVPL,f},&
T_{pord}\dot P_{ord}&=\mathrm{sat}(\kappa P_{ref},P_{min},P_{max})-P_{ord},\\
T_{iq}\dot I_{q,cmd,f}&=I_{q,cmd}-I_{q,cmd,f},
\end{align}
where $\dot P_{ord}$ is additionally rate-limited to $[\dot P_{min},\dot P_{max}]$. The REEC\_A
command pair divides the order by the filtered voltage and adds the voltage-dip reactive injection,
\begin{align}
I_{p,0}&=\frac{P_{ord}}{\max\left(V_{t,f},0.01\right)}, &
I_{q,0}&=\frac{\kappa Q_{ref}}{\max\left(V_{t,f},0.01\right)}+I_{q,inj},\\
I_{q,inj}&=\mathrm{sat}\!\left(K_{qv}\,\mathrm{db}\!\left(V_{ref,0}-V_{t,f}\right),I_{ql1},I_{qh1}\right)
&&\text{active only if }V_{t,f}<V_{dip}\text{ or }V_{t,f}>V_{up},
\end{align}
followed by the active-priority allocation
\[
I_p=\mathrm{sat}\!\left(I_{p,0},\,0,\,I_{max}\right),\qquad
|I_q|\le\min\!\left(I_{max},\sqrt{I_{max}^2-I_p^2}\right).
\]
The regulated currents then track their commands through the converter lag. The active-current
state uses
\[
\dot I_{p,reg}=\min\!\left(\frac{I_p^{tgt}-I_{p,reg}}{T_g},r_{rpwr}\right).
\]
For the reactive-current state, first define
$d_q=(I_{q,cmd,f}-I_{q,reg})/T_g$; then the implementation uses
$d_q\leftarrow\min(d_q,I_{qr,max})$ when $Q_{ref}>0$,
$d_q\leftarrow\max(d_q,I_{qr,min})$ when $Q_{ref}<0$, and leaves $d_q$ unchanged when
$Q_{ref}=0$, followed by $\dot I_{q,reg}=d_q$. Thus these are source-mapped one-sided limits, not an
unconditional symmetric interval clamp. Here $I_p^{tgt}$ is $I_p$ passed through the LVPL
characteristic when that switch is enabled and floored at zero below $V_{lvpnt0}$. The $0.01$~pu
division guard is
\texttt{NUMERICAL\_METHOD}; every gain, time constant, and limit above is
\texttt{SOURCE\_DEFINED}.

\subsection{Mode dispatch}
In GFL mode the active indices are $14{:}20$ and $\dot x_{1:13}=0$; in GFM mode the active indices
are $1{:}13$ and $\dot x_{14:20}=0$; in the tripped mode all twenty derivatives are zero and the
injection is identically zero. Only the active branch enters the dynamic solve, and the active-index
set is queried from the device rather than hard-coded, so the layout above is not duplicated in the
integrator. Atomic state mapping is followed by a full-KCL right-limit solve; a failed solve rolls
the transaction back.

\section{Switching formulation: what is solved at a mode change}
\label{sec:switching-formulation}
This section states the mathematics executed at every switching instant. It is the governing
formulation for the SG trip, the GFL$\leftrightarrow$GFM transfers, and the SG reclose.

\subsection{The plant is a DAE, not an ODE}
Between events the plant is a semi-explicit index-1 differential--algebraic system, not a plain
ordinary differential equation:
\begin{align}
\dot x&=f_q(x,y,u),\label{eq:sw-diff}\\
0&=g_q(x,y,u)=Y_q V-I_{inj}(x,y).\label{eq:sw-alg}
\end{align}
Here the stored vector $x\in\mathbb R^{86}$ is the fixed inventory of six SG electromagnetic
coordinates and four 20-state IBR supersets. This stored dimension is \emph{not} the differential
Newton order. The exact $T'_{q0}=0$ limit freezes SG coordinate $E'_d$, so the SG contributes five
active electromagnetic coordinates. Each online IBR contributes either seven active GFL
coordinates or thirteen active GFM coordinates, as returned by its current mode. Therefore the
active differential order is 33 in the all-GFL configuration and 57 in the all-GFM configuration;
only these active coordinates are included in the trapezoidal residual. The two prime-mover states
of Section~\ref{sec:dyn} are advanced by the separate exact update~\eqref{eq:gov-exact} and enter
\eqref{eq:sw-diff} through the input $u$, not as Newton unknowns. Accordingly $u$ carries $T_m$ and
$E_{fd}$, $y$ stores rectangular bus voltages, and $q$ is the \emph{discrete mode}: the tuple of
network topology, per-device mode, and breaker status. Equation~\eqref{eq:sw-alg} is the full
all-KCL residual over all $2n_b$ real rows; a single module owns it, while each device contributes
only its own slice of $f_q$ and its own $I_{inj}$.

The discretisation is the implicit trapezoidal rule, so a normal step is not the explicit
recursion $x_{k+1}=x_k+h\,f(\cdot)$ but the simultaneous nonlinear system
\begin{align}
x_{k+1}-x_k-\tfrac{h}{2}\left[f_q(x_k,y_k,u)+f_q(x_{k+1},y_{k+1},u)\right]&=0,\label{eq:sw-trap}\\
g_q(x_{k+1},y_{k+1},u)&=0,\label{eq:sw-trapalg}
\end{align}
solved together for the active differential coordinates of $x_{k+1}$ and all algebraic coordinates
$y_{k+1}$. The composite 200-s route does \emph{not} use a fixed count of three corrector sweeps:
it forms the coupled trapezoidal/full-KCL residual and applies the project Newton solver until the
residual tolerance is met, otherwise subdividing the attempted step and ultimately failing closed if
the accepted-leaf gate cannot be met. Both $f_q$ and $g_q$ carry the subscript $q$: they are the
equations \emph{of the present discrete mode}. A switching event changes $q$, hence changes the
equations and the active-coordinate set themselves.

\subsection{The reference-role handover: SG to IBR}
Before the mechanical detail below, four conceptual points must be settled, because this is where
the formulation is most easily misunderstood: \emph{the SG and the IBRs obey different equations,
so how can an IBR ``replace'' the SG?}

\subsubsection{The SG and the IBRs really are different DAEs}
The governing branch equations were stated once in Section~\ref{sec:dyn}; they are not repeated here.
The important distinction is that the SG has real inertia $H$, rotor angle $\delta$, and mechanical
input $T_m$ supplied by its prime-mover state $P_m$, whereas the GFM branch has virtual inertia, the synthetic angle
$\delta_{VSM}=\delta_{PLL}+\delta_{IT}$, and $P_{ref,inv}$. The GFL branch has no swing equation at
all; it is a current source. Hence no state is shared and \emph{no equation sets
$\omega_{SG}=\omega_m$}; each frequency variable belongs to its own device. The devices replace one
another only in the supervisory reference-setting role described next, not by transforming one ODE
into another.

\subsubsection{The reference is a role, not an equation transformation}
In the time-domain DAE (Section~\ref{sec:switching-formulation}, \S 6.1) \textbf{every bus is an
unknown of the all-KCL residual} $0=Y_qV-I_{bus}$ over all $2n_b$ real rows; no bus angle is pinned
as an algebraic slack. The code never replaces a KCL row by a pinned angle, and
\path{reference_owner_schema.m} states it directly: ``reference ownership is
supervisory/physical, NOT a KCL-row replacement.'' The reference is therefore \emph{not a bus
type} but a role played by whichever device controls the island's angle and frequency: while the SG
is online the role belongs to the SG; while it is open it belongs to IBR1 (bus 2) through its GFM
branch. What ``transforms'' at $t=20$~s is \textbf{not the SG equations into IBR equations} but the
handover of the reference-setting role from one device to another.

\subsubsection{Three things change together at the handover instant}
The mechanical detail lives in Section~\ref{sec:sw-inverse}; at the role level, three changes happen
inside a single transaction:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{SG:} the equations switch to the open-circuit mode --- $I_d=I_q=0$, $T_e=0$, flux
decay, the rotor coasts as $2H\,\Delta\dot\omega=T_m(t)-D\Delta\omega$ under the declared
diagnostic synchroniser input, and \emph{all six states stay in the state vector}
\item \textbf{IBR:} the reset map switches GFL$\to$GFM by initialising the GFM branch so the
\emph{terminal current is continuous}, $I^+=I^-$ --- the inverse problem of
Section~\ref{sec:sw-inverse}
\item \textbf{Network:} the matrix $Y$ changes (the SG breaker opens) and the KCL is re-solved
for $y^+$
\end{enumerate}
All three are committed together as one transaction, so there is no interval in which the island is
``without a reference'' (the supervisor plot keeps $GRA=1$ throughout). The simultaneous GFM switch
of all four IBRs at 20~s is therefore the result of this coordinated transaction, not of the four
AGSI indices crossing their thresholds at the same instant.

\subsubsection{$H$, $\omega$, $f$ differ --- why are they interchangeable}
This is the deepest point, and the answer is \emph{we never equate them directly}. The coupling is
not an equation between frequency variables; it is the network. Every device injects current into
the same set of buses, so KCL forces the bus voltages into geometric consistency; at steady state
all voltages rotate at the same frequency, and each device locks to that frequency by its own
mechanism --- the SG by its physical rotor (integrating $\dot\delta=\omega_0\Delta\omega$), the IBR
by a PLL driving $v_q\to0$ to align with the terminal-voltage angle (the WECC GFL uses $\angle V$
directly, algebraically). \emph{Steady-state synchronism is therefore emergent from the network,
not a directly imposed condition.}

This is why the switch must be ``continuous at the terminal'', not ``continuous in the state'': the
terminal current is what the network sees, so keeping $I$ from jumping is exactly not disturbing the
network's balance. The internal variables ($\delta$ of the SG, $\omega_m,\delta_{VSM}$ of the GFM)
belong to each device and may be re-initialised freely as long as the terminal effect stays the
same.

\subsection{The transition at a switching instant}
Equations~\eqref{eq:sw-trap}--\eqref{eq:sw-trapalg} are \emph{not} continued across an event. At a
switching instant $t_e$ the trajectory undergoes a hybrid-DAE transition in three ordered stages:
\begin{align}
&q^-\;\longrightarrow\;q^+ &&\text{discrete jump},\label{eq:sw-jump}\\
&x(t_e^+)=R_{q^-\to q^+}\!\left(x(t_e^-),y(t_e^-)\right) &&\text{state reset map},\label{eq:sw-reset}\\
&0=g_{q^+}\!\left(x(t_e^+),y(t_e^+),u\right)\;\;\text{solve for }y(t_e^+) &&\text{right-limit algebraic solve}.\label{eq:sw-right}
\end{align}
Stage~\eqref{eq:sw-jump} selects the post-event admittance matrix $Y_{q^+}$ directly from the
declared topology set by its identifier, and commits the device-mode and breaker maps as a single
transaction: the whole update is validated before any field is written, and a failed validation
leaves the state identical to its input. Stage~\eqref{eq:sw-reset} is described below.
Stage~\eqref{eq:sw-right} solves \emph{only} the algebraic block, by damped Newton iteration with a
backtracking line search, holding $x$ fixed at $x(t_e^+)$ and accepting the result only if the KCL
residual meets the step tolerance; otherwise the whole transaction rolls back. Differential
integration then resumes from $\left(x(t_e^+),y(t_e^+)\right)$ at the next step.

Consequently two samples exist at $t_e$: a left limit and a right limit. The tables and figures in
this report store the right limit.

\subsection{The reset map is terminal continuity, not state copying}
The key structural property is that the \textbf{stored state inventory never changes}. Each IBR is
a constant-dimension 20-state superset in which the GFM states $1{:}13$ and the GFL states
$14{:}20$ always exist. A mode change re-selects which sub-block is \emph{active}; the inactive
block is held exactly, $\dot x_{inactive}=0$, under a frozen inactive-state rule
(\texttt{PROJECT\_DERIVED}: continuity plus algebraic-residual minimisation). The full stored
trajectory therefore retains 86 coordinates, but the reduced Newton residual and its Jacobian use
the mode-dependent active set and do change order when a 7-state GFL branch is exchanged for a
13-state GFM branch. No state is structurally deleted, which is why later transfer and SG reclose
remain meaningful.

The reset map $R$ in~\eqref{eq:sw-reset} transfers \emph{physical terminal conditions}, not state
coordinates, and is owned by the device:
\begin{align}
V_{bus}&=y_{2b-1}+\mathrm jy_{2b}, &
I^-&=I_{inj}\!\left(x^-,y,u,q^-\right),\\
S^-&=V_{bus}\,\overline{I^-}, & P^-&=\Re S^-,\quad Q^-=\Im S^-,\\
x^+\big|_{target}&=\mathcal I_{q^+}\!\left(V_{bus},P^-,Q^-\right), &
x^+\big|_{inactive}&=x^-\big|_{inactive},
\end{align}
followed by the fail-closed acceptance test
\begin{equation}
\left|I_{inj}\!\left(x^+,y,u,q^+\right)-I^-\right|\le10^{-10}.\label{eq:sw-cont}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal I_{q^+}$ is the target branch's own equilibrium/transfer initialiser. The invariant
carried across the switch is therefore the terminal pair $(V,I)$ --- equivalently $(P,Q)$ --- and
not the state vector. No PLL angle is copied out of the GFL branch, because the GFL branch owns no
PLL state; the GFM angle is re-derived from $V_{bus}$ and $I^-$. The bus index $b$ is resolved from
the device's registered bus position, so the map contains no hard-coded bus identity.

\subsection{Why the recursion becomes an assignment, and what equation\break is solved}
\label{sec:sw-inverse}
This subsection answers the most direct question: during a normal step we advance the recursion
$x_{k+1}=x_k+\frac{h}{2}[f_q(x_k)+f_q(x_{k+1})]$, yet at a switching instant the code appears to
simply \emph{assign} $x^+=(\text{a value})$. What equation is being solved, and what makes the two
sides of the event sync with each other?

\subsubsection{Why the recursion cannot be continued}
Write the step that straddles the switching time $t_e$ explicitly, with $t_k<t_e<t_{k+1}$. The
trapezoidal rule requires
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}-x_k=\frac{h}{2}\Big[\underbrace{f_{q^-}(x_k,y_k)}_{\text{pre-switch mode}}
+\underbrace{f_{q^+}(x_{k+1},y_{k+1})}_{\text{post-switch mode}}\Big].
\label{eq:sw-illegal}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:sw-illegal} is \emph{mathematically invalid}, because the trapezoidal rule is
derived from $\int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}}f\,dt$ on the assumption that $f$ is \emph{continuous} on that
interval; at $t_e$ the vector field jumps ($f_{q^-}\ne f_{q^+}$), so the order of accuracy collapses
and the result approximates the integral of no field at all. The correct treatment is to
\textbf{split the step at $t_e$} and treat $t_e$ as the boundary of two subproblems: the one on
$[t_k,t_e]$ uses $f_{q^-}$ and the one on $[t_e,t_{k+1}]$ uses $f_{q^+}$, and each subproblem
\emph{needs its own initial condition}. The initial condition of the second subproblem is
$x(t_e^+)$, which is exactly what the reset map must produce. The recursion is therefore not
``gone'' --- it \emph{ends one round and starts another}, with an initial-condition assignment in
between.

\subsubsection{The equation actually solved at the switching instant}
$x^+$ is not a value chosen out of thin air; it is the \emph{solution of an inverse problem} with
an explicit governing equation. Let $\mathcal C_{q}(x,V)$ be the terminal current-injection
function of mode $q$. The problem is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\text{find }x^+\ \text{such that}\quad
\mathcal C_{q^+}\!\left(x^+,V_{bus}\right)-\underbrace{\mathcal C_{q^-}\!\left(x^-,V_{bus}\right)}_{=\,I^-\ \text{already known}}=0\;}
\label{eq:sw-inverse}
\end{equation}
subject to the constraint that the inactive block keeps its values,
$x^+\big|_{inactive}=x^-\big|_{inactive}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:sw-inverse} is the equation that is
really ``solved''. In physical terms: \emph{the controller may change its operating law instantly,
but the current leaving the terminal must not jump}, because that current is tied to the energy
stored in the filter inductances, which cannot change as a step without an infinite voltage. The
continuity check~\eqref{eq:sw-cont} on $|I^+-I^-|\le10^{-10}$ is therefore not a generic numerical
quality check; it is \textbf{a verification that the inverse problem~\eqref{eq:sw-inverse} was
actually solved}. If the value exceeds the threshold, no consistent $x^+$ exists and the
transaction must not commit.

\subsubsection{The closed-form inversion: GFL$\to$GFM step by step}
The GFL$\to$GFM case at 20~s is the best example because it is solvable \emph{in closed form}
with no iteration at all. Every step below is a direct back-substitution into the GFM branch
equations.

\textbf{Step 1 --- read the terminal condition from the left side.} Compute the current the GFL
branch is actually injecting, using the GFL injection~\eqref{eq:gfl-current}, and convert it to
power:
\begin{equation}
I^-=\frac{\left(I_{p,reg}\gamma(|V|)-jI_{q,reg}\right)e^{j\angle V}}{\kappa},\qquad
S^-=V_{bus}\overline{I^-},\qquad P^-=\Re S^-,\quad Q^-=\Im S^-.
\end{equation}
The three quantities $(V_{bus},P^-,Q^-)$ are \emph{all} that is carried across the event; no GFL
state variable is forwarded.

\textbf{Step 2 --- find the required internal voltage.} The GFM branch injects current through the
impedance $Z_{sys}=\kappa(R_e+jX_L)$ by~\eqref{eq:gfm-current}, so for it to deliver exactly
$I^-$ the voltage behind the impedance must be
\begin{equation}
E_{terminal}=V_{bus}+Z_{sys}I^-
\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;
E_{VSM}=\left|E_{terminal}\right|,\quad
\delta_{VSM}=\angle E_{terminal}.
\label{eq:sw-inv-E}
\end{equation}
This is the direct \emph{inversion} of~\eqref{eq:gfm-current}: originally we have $E$ and find
$I$; now we have $I$ and find $E$. The equation solves immediately because it is linear in $I$.

\textbf{Step 3 --- decompose $E_{VSM},\delta_{VSM}$ back into state variables.} We now have what the
\emph{combination} must be, and still have to split it into individual states. Start with the
angle: the PLL is locked to the terminal-voltage angle ($\delta_{PLL}=\angle V_{bus}$, positive
$d$-axis), so from $\delta_{VSM}=\delta_{PLL}+\delta_{IT}$,
\begin{equation}
\delta_{IT}=\operatorname{wrap}_{\pi}\!\left(\angle E_{terminal}-\angle V_{bus}\right),
\qquad x_{PLL}=0,\quad \Delta\omega_m=0,\quad x_w=0.
\label{eq:sw-inv-angle}
\end{equation}
For the magnitude, invert the voltage-PI equation~\eqref{eq:gfm-vpi} to solve for the integrator:
\begin{align}
E_{VSM}&=V_{ref}-m_qQ_f+k_{pv}\left(V_{ref}-V_f\right)+k_{iv}x_E\notag\\
&\Longrightarrow\quad
x_E=\frac{E_{VSM}-V_{ref}+m_qQ_f-k_{pv}\left(V_{ref}-V_f\right)}{k_{iv}},
\label{eq:sw-inv-xE}
\end{align}
which requires $k_{iv}>0$ (the code checks this and fails closed otherwise). The five filter states
are set exactly to the measured quantities at that instant ($P_{inv,f}=\kappa P^-$,
$Q_{inv,f}=\kappa Q^-$, $V_{inv,f}=|V_{bus}|$, and $I_{d,f},I_{q,f}$ from projecting the current
onto the PLL frame), because a freshly restarted filter must not create a spurious transient from
an initial value that disagrees with its input.

\textbf{Step 4 --- verify the solution lies in the source-allowed region.} Before accepting $x^+$
the code checks three layers and fails closed on each: $|I^-|$ must not exceed $I_{max}^F/\kappa$
(if it did, the clamp would change $P,Q$, which is not acceptable), $E_{VSM}$ must lie in
$[E_{min},E_{max}]$ of~\eqref{eq:gfm-elim}, and $\delta_{IT}$ must lie in
$[\delta_{IT,min},\delta_{IT,max}]$. It also rejects the case where $|I^-|$ lands exactly on the
clamp boundary, because the derivative is discontinuous there and the solution is not unique.

\textbf{Step 5 --- verify by recomputing.} Feed $x^+$ back into the GFM current equation and
compare with $I^-$ by~\eqref{eq:sw-cont}. This confirms steps 2--3 contain no error. Theoretically
the value must be exactly zero; the $10^{-10}$ tolerance therefore covers only floating-point
round-off, not a physical relaxation.

\subsubsection{Device-level sync versus system-level sync}
The word ``sync'' is used at two levels with two different mechanisms.

\textbf{Device level} is steps 1--5 above: make the device itself consistent with the terminal
condition at the \emph{same} voltage $V_{bus}$. Note that throughout steps 1--5 the voltage
$V_{bus}$ is held fixed and never moves, so this is a per-device local problem that can be solved
one device at a time.

\textbf{System level} is the right-limit solve~\eqref{eq:sw-right}, needed because when several
devices change their laws at once (and/or the topology changes $Y_{q^-}\to Y_{q^+}$), the old
voltage $y(t_e^-)$ is in general \emph{no longer consistent} with the new KCL:
\begin{equation}
g_{q^+}\!\left(x^+,y(t_e^-),u\right)=Y_{q^+}V(t_e^-)-I_{bus}\!\left(x^+,y(t_e^-)\right)\ne0.
\end{equation}
One must therefore solve for $y(t_e^+)$ anew, \emph{holding $x^+$ fixed}, by damped Newton
\begin{equation}
J_{yy}\,\Delta y^{(i)}=-g_{q^+}\!\left(x^+,y^{(i)},u\right),\qquad
y^{(i+1)}=y^{(i)}+\lambda_i\Delta y^{(i)},\qquad
J_{yy}=\frac{\partial g_{q^+}}{\partial y},
\label{eq:sw-newton}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_i\in(0,1]$ comes from a backtracking line search that shrinks until the residual
norm actually decreases (preventing over-long steps from diverging). It starts from
$y^{(0)}=y(t_e^-)$ and stops when $\|g_{q^+}\|_\infty\le$ the step tolerance; if it does not
converge, the whole transaction rolls back.

The order \textbf{device first, then network} is essential and cannot be reversed: the device step
needs a known $V_{bus}$ to invert its equations, and the network step needs a known $x^+$ to
assemble $I_{bus}$. The two steps are therefore one transaction that commits or rolls back
together.

After this the system has a pair $\left(x(t_e^+),y(t_e^+)\right)$ consistent with both the device
equations and KCL, and the trapezoidal recursion restarts correctly with $f_{q^+}$ as the vector
field and this pair as the initial condition,
\begin{equation}
x_{k+2}-x(t_e^+)=\frac{h'}{2}\left[f_{q^+}\!\left(x(t_e^+),y(t_e^+)\right)
+f_{q^+}\!\left(x_{k+2},y_{k+2}\right)\right],\qquad h'=t_{k+2}-t_e.
\end{equation}
In short, \emph{the recursion never crosses the event; it is cut into two segments joined by a
physical continuity condition, not by continuity of the state coordinates.}

\subsubsection{The SG case: why an extra synchronism gate is needed}
The IBR case above has a ``sync'' that solves immediately in closed form, because the converter may
freely choose its internal angle ($\delta_{IT}$ is just a number in the state). The SG \emph{cannot}
do this: the rotor angle $\delta$ is tied to a real rotating mass and cannot change
instantaneously. When closing the SG breaker we therefore have \emph{no freedom} to invert an
equation for a desired $\delta^+$; whatever $\delta$ happens to be must simply be accepted.

The roles are thus completely reversed: instead of \emph{solving} for the continuity condition, we
must \emph{wait} until the physics brings the system close enough to continuity on its own. That is
the job of the gate~\eqref{eq:guard-margin}, which compares the two phasors on either side of the
breaker and closes only when the difference is small enough in magnitude, frequency, and angle. The
accepted-run guard values reported with the final trajectory are evidence that the condition was made
true before closing, not enforced after.

This is the mathematical explanation of why the 145.000~s close request need not close
immediately: \emph{the breaker closure is an event with no supporting reset map}, so it must wait
for the state to approach continuity by itself, whereas an IBR mode switch has a solvable reset
map and happens the instant the transaction commands it.

\subsection{The SG trip and the gated reclose}
At the trip the SG states are \emph{not} removed from $x$. The breaker-open branch
replaces the connected-stator algebra with open-circuit physics: $I_d=I_q=0$, $T_e=0$, and
\begin{align}
2H\Delta\dot\omega&=T_m(t)-D\Delta\omega, &
T'_{d0}\dot E'_q&=E_{fd}(t)+c_dE''_q-d_dE'_q,\\
T'_{q0}\dot E'_d&=c_qE''_d-d_qE'_d, &
T''_{d0}\dot E''_q&=E'_q-E''_q,\\
&&T''_{q0}\dot E''_d&=E'_d-E''_d.
\end{align}
The current-dependent flux terms vanish and $\dot\delta=\omega_0\Delta\omega$ continues; the
$T'_{q0}=0$ singular limit still holds $E'_d=0$. Here $T_m(t)$ is supplied by the offline exact
prime-mover update using the deterministic phase-planner command, while the diagnostic voltage
matcher supplies $E_{fd}(t)$. Neither input is claimed to be a frozen pre-trip constant over the
whole outage. The rotor and open-circuit flux states therefore continue under the declared
diagnostic preparation, which makes later resynchronisation meaningful.

The reclose is \emph{gated}, not scheduled. A guard compares the network bus phasor against the SG
open-circuit EMF $\left(E''_q-\mathrm jE''_d\right)e^{\mathrm j\delta}$ and requires
\begin{equation}
|\Delta V|\le0.05~\text{pu},\qquad
|\Delta f|\le0.001~\text{pu},\qquad
|\Delta\theta|\le10^\circ,
\end{equation}
expressed as the signed margin
\begin{equation}
m=\min\left(0.05-|\Delta V|,\;0.001-|\Delta f|,\;10^\circ-|\Delta\theta_{wrap}|\right)\ge0,
\label{eq:guard-margin}
\end{equation}
sustained for a $0.5$~s dwell. The requested close time is therefore only the earliest instant at
which closure \emph{may} occur; the accepted close time is an output of the guard and dwell, not a
scheduled constant.

\section{Power-flow operating point}
The power-flow is solved by the project Newton--Raphson routine. Q-limit
enforcement is switched on, and the generator reactive-capability limits behind
it are \emph{source-defined}, not assumed or tuned: they are the IEEE14
generator data $Q_{\max}$ and $Q_{\min}$ in MVAr
(\path{cases.case_matpower6_case14}, \texttt{mpc.gen} columns 5 and 4),
converted to per unit with the single system base $S_{\mathrm{base}}=100$~MVA,
\begin{equation}
Q_{\max,\mathrm{pu}}=\frac{Q_{\max,\mathrm{MVAr}}}{100},\qquad
Q_{\min,\mathrm{pu}}=\frac{Q_{\min,\mathrm{MVAr}}}{100},
\label{eq:qlimits}
\end{equation}
with $P_{\max}$ taken the same way from \texttt{mpc.gen} column 9. Buses
carrying no finite generator limit are left unconstrained
($Q_{\max}=+\infty$, $Q_{\min}=-\infty$) and print a dash.

It must be stated plainly that \emph{enabling the mechanism is not the same as
constraining this operating point}. The PV$\to$PQ Q-limit switch is defined
only for PV buses, and after the inverter buses are mapped to PQ resources this
case has no PV bus at all, so the switch resolves in zero rounds and changes
nothing. Two consequences follow and are reported rather than masked: the slack
bus 1 settles at $Q_g=-0.196$~pu against its registered $Q_{\min}=0$, and bus 6
at $Q_g=0.2476$~pu against $Q_{\max}=0.24$. Neither is a solver error --- a REF
bus absorbs whatever reactive power closes the network balance and a PQ resource
injects its scheduled $Q$ --- but neither is a limit the solve enforced. The
limit columns therefore report registered source data, not satisfied
constraints. The enforcement path itself is exercised by the baseline IEEE14
case, which does carry PV buses.

The admissible voltage band is $\pm5\%$ about the $1.00$~pu slack reference,
i.e.\ $V_{\max}=1.05$~pu and $V_{\min}=0.95$~pu on every bus
(\texttt{CASE\_DEFINED} in \texttt{case\_ieee14bus\_eecon49\_switch}). This is
a reporting and admissibility band, not a PF equation input: the slack is the
only voltage-controlled bus in this operating point, so the band does not act
as a PV-setpoint clamp. One observation is reported rather than masked: bus 6
solves at $1.0575$~pu, \mbox{$7.5\times10^{-3}$~pu} above $V_{\max}$, because
its mapped IBR6 reactive dispatch and the fixed 5--6 tap ratio are
source/case-defined and were not adjusted to satisfy the band.

The bus roles (REF/PQ/GFL) in the Type column are the \texttt{bus\_role}
labelling descriptor (Section~\ref{sec:case-bus-roles}); being a label only,
they do not alter \eqref{eq:qlimits} or any solved quantity.
